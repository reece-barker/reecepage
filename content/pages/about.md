## Where it started

I started primary school in West Sussex, on the south coast of England, before my family moved to Munich. That is
where I grew up, at Munich International School, and where German went from a school subject to a second language I
now use at work.

I wrote my first lines of code at twelve. At fifteen I shipped my first real product, a real time extension for a
web framework, and recorded a thirty minute walkthrough explaining exactly how it worked.

## Geography changed everything

For my IB Diploma I took Computer Science, German and Geography at higher level. It was Geography that changed
everything. My Geography teacher made the subject come alive: she showed me how the planet actually works, and how
much is at stake. Without her, I would not be doing what I do today.

My extended essay looked at London's Ultra Low Emission Zone, and whether it really cut CO2. It was the first time my
two interests met: data, and the environment.

## Freelance at sixteen

At sixteen I went freelance. Over almost three years I built full stack web platforms with secure logins and live
monitoring. I also built a [support and ticketing system](/projects/support-ticketing-system) for online communities in
the entertainment space, connected to the APIs they already relied on, and sold it commercially on two marketplaces.
For the flow meter manufacturer Bronkhorst, I built a [customer configurator](/projects/bronkhorst-configurator) that
checks real physics constraints as you type. I delivered it in a week.

## Munich International School

Alongside all that, I spent three summers in the IT department at Munich International School. Today I am back there
on contract, supporting IT and building on Microsoft's Power Platform, including the
[purchase order system](/projects/mis-purchase-orders) the finance team works with. I also built my own browser
extension for the IT team, [Scan Assistant](/projects/scan-assistant), to scan the school's iPads in and out, match
them against class lists and turn it all into a report automatically.

## Saxion

In 2024 I moved to the Netherlands to study Applied Computer Science at Saxion University of Applied Sciences in
Enschede. My first year ended with my propaedeutic diploma, cum laude. The degree goes deep: embedded C and C++, real
time operating systems, signal processing and machine learning. In my first year I helped build an autonomous robot
that explores a maze and finds the shortest way out on its own.

In one project my two old interests met again. I built a Raspberry Pi that
[listens to passing traffic and profiles it by sound alone](/projects/traffic-sound-profiling), to understand emissions
without a single camera. It scored nine out of ten.

At Saxion's International Project Week I earned the highest distinction. Now I am in the Industrial Automation minor,
where a team of thirteen is designing a [full production line](/projects/lego-bulldozer-production-line) with eight ABB
robots, vision systems and PLCs, to assemble a LEGO bulldozer every five minutes. Alongside it I am helping build a
[machine that sorts loose LEGO parts](/projects/lego-sorting-machine) by colour, by size and with a camera that
recognises each piece.

## The Honours Programme

I was also selected for the Saxion Honours Programme, a track for students who want more than the standard
curriculum. For me it has been about people as much as projects: working with students from completely different
fields, building a real social circle, learning skills far outside computer science, and asking bigger questions about
who I am and what actually matters.

One moment stood out. My lecture on sustainable software, "Smart Tech, Greener Planet", was the highlight my peers kept
coming back to.

## The startup

Out of Saxion also came [my startup](/projects/microclimate-startup), co-founded with three classmates. The idea starts
with something simple. Every room has a climate: temperature, humidity, air quality and light. Our AI connects to the
systems a building already has and controls them together, adjusting to who is actually there.

At home, that is comfort with less wasted energy. In industry, it is stable conditions for people, products and
machines. And in healthcare, where it matters most, it is a room tuned to each patient, to support better care and
recovery.

## How I use AI

I belong to the first generation to grow up alongside modern AI. I use large language models every day, to build, to
research and to learn faster. Using them that much has taught me exactly where they fall short. They can be
confidently wrong. They need clear limits, and people who check the work.

That is why I want to help build it well. I believe AI can do real good for the planet and for society, from cutting
wasted energy to improving care, as long as the people building it choose that path on purpose.

## People and faith

Ask the people around me what I am like and you will hear the same few things: I am easy to be around, I am funny,
and I am someone you can count on. I am close with my parents, and I keep a circle of friends I trust completely.

Underneath all of it is my faith. You see it in how I work and how I treat people: being honest
when it is inconvenient, being patient, doing good work even when nobody is checking, and helping people because it is
the right thing to do.

## What I want next

It is simple to say, and harder to do. I want to work on projects that genuinely make people's lives better, and leave
the planet in better shape than I found it. Software, hardware, automation, AI: for me they are all tools for the same
goal.

Next comes a European Project Semester at TH Augsburg, then an internship and a graduation project in industry. If you
